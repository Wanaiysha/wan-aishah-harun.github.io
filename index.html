PhD, Nuclear Astrophysics

I study how the elements are forged inside massive stars with nucleosynthesis modelling, convective shell burning, and the shell mergers that reshape a star's structure in the final years before core collapse. My work sits between nuclear reaction physics and stellar evolution: the reaction rates set deep in a star's interior, and what they mean for the star as a whole.

Research

Nuclear astrophysics at the interface of reaction physics and stellar structure — how nuclear reactions add up to the life, and death, of a massive star.

Nucleosynthesis

Tracking how successive burning stages build up the elements, from hydrogen fusion through to the iron-group nuclei formed up to the core collapse.

Massive stars

Modelling the structure and evolution of stars massive enough to burn all the way to silicon and iron, and what ultimately sends them to core collapse.

Shell mergers

Studying convective burning shells that merge in the final burning stages — episodes that can reshuffle composition and change a star's pre-supernova structure.

Modelling

Building and applying stellar and reaction-network models to connect nuclear physics inputs to observable stellar outcomes.

Reaction

Astrophysical reaction of interest:
Projectile a + Target X → Ejectile Y + Recoil b

For each reaction we compute mass m (amu), mass–energy mc² (MeV), mass excess Δ (MeV), the reaction Q-value, the Coulomb barrier, and the Gamow peak energy E0, width ΔE0, and Gamow energy EG.

Q-value (mass excesses for neutral atoms):
Q = Σ Δᵢ (initial) − Σ Δf (final)  [MeV]

Coulomb barrier:
VC ≈ 1.44 · Z₁Z₂ / r  [MeV],   r ≈ r₀(∛A₁ + ∛A₂)  [fm],   r₀ ≈ 1.3 fm
